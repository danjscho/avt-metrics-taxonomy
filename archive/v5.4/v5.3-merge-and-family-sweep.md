## PART A: Merge-Candidate Analysis

### 1. GV.SG-18 (PCCP Documentation Completeness) vs GV.CR-9 (FDA PCCP-Equivalent Pre-Defined Acceptance Criteria)

**Verdict:** Merge as parent + sub-parts (keep separate sections; document relationship)

**Rationale:**

- **GV.CR-9** tests substantive quality of acceptance criteria (do the thresholds meaningfully limit change risk?). Definition: "Performance acceptance thresholds pre-specified and quantitative; Regression test suite defined; Fairness/equity criteria included; Rollback procedure specified."
- **GV.SG-18** tests structural completeness of the documented PCCP (is the plan comprehensive per regulatory regime?). Definition: "PCCP scope statement; quantitative thresholds; regression suite; fairness/equity criteria; rollback procedure; audit-trail commitment."

Both metrics share four substantive criteria (quantitative thresholds, regression suite, fairness/equity, rollback), but GV.SG-18 adds scope-statement enumeration and audit-trail commitment—structural elements GV.CR-9 doesn't test. The taxonomy already flagged this: GV.SG-18's Limitations section states "Future minor release may merge them under a parent + sub-parts framing if reviewer confirms the overlap is excessive."

**Recommendation:** Propose GV.CR-9 as parent (substantive quality gate) and GV.SG-18 as sub-part (structural completeness gate). Both remain distinct metrics; the relationship should be explicitly named in one or both bodies. No renumbering required.

### 2. GV.PD-12 (Training Data Representativeness Documentation) vs GV.PD-7 (Training Data Inclusion Status)

**Verdict:** Distinct (keep separate)

**Rationale:**

- **GV.PD-7** answers: "Is deployer data flowing into vendor training pipelines? Yes / No / Derived?" Binary disclosure + documentation of which components (ASR, summariser, coder) and which data types (audio, transcripts, notes).
- **GV.PD-12** assumes answer to GV.PD-7 is "Yes" or "Derived", then asks: "Does the training data *cover* the intended patient population?" Documentation of demographic strata (age, ethnicity, accent, comorbidity), clinical-setting mix, known under-representation, and mitigation plan.

GV.PD-12's "Why this tier?" section explicitly states it is "the foundational pre-condition for downstream bias-mitigation work" and "a deployer cannot defensibly run [downstream equity metrics] without first knowing whether the training data could plausibly support equivalent performance across strata." GV.PD-7 is transparency (what flows where), GV.PD-12 is fairness-readiness (whether the flow covers the population). Distinct constructs, distinct governance paths.

**Recommendation:** Keep separate. The relationship is sequential (GV.PD-7 must answer before GV.PD-12 is meaningful) but not parent-sub-part.

---

### 3. GV.PD-14 (SAR Deletion-Pause Interaction) vs GV.PD-2 (Audio Time-to-Deletion) + GV.PD-10 (Subject Access Request Fulfilment)

**Verdict:** Distinct; GV.PD-14 tests interaction (keep separate)

**Rationale:**

- **GV.PD-2** measures the deletion timeline under routine (non-SAR) conditions: median time-to-deletion, P95, P99, compliance with policy threshold (typically 24 hours).
- **GV.PD-10** tests the ability to fulfil SAR requests: locate all data, export in usable format, within 30 days.
- **GV.PD-14** tests a specific edge-case *interaction*: "When a SAR is opened on a patient with active AVT-derived data approaching the GV.PD-2 deletion threshold, deletion is paused for the duration of the SAR and resumes only after formal SAR close."

GV.PD-14's "Formal Definition" requires scenario walk-through: (1) Open synthetic SAR on test patient approaching GV.PD-2 threshold; (2) Verify deletion paused; (3) Verify SAR response includes AVT data; (4) Close SAR; (5) Verify deletion resumes from SAR-close within GV.PD-2 cadence. This is a behavioral contract between two independent processes—neither GV.PD-2 nor GV.PD-10 alone tests this interaction.

**Recommendation:** Keep separate. GV.PD-14 is a mandatory integration test (all three should be measured) rather than a merger candidate.

### 4. GV.PD-15 (Right-to-Restrict Tooling Support) vs GV.PD-11 (Right to Erasure Compliance)

**Verdict:** Distinct; different legal constructs (keep separate)

**Rationale:**

- **GV.PD-11** (Article 17): Data is deleted or irreversibly de-identified. Three outcome classes: deletable, anonymisable, technically-irreversible.
- **GV.PD-15** (Article 18): Data is marked, retained, but excluded from processing. Five criteria: restrict flag present and distinguishable from erase flag; restricted records excluded from training/analytics/re-processing; restricted records remain accessible for evidence/SAR/audit; reversal procedure documented; audit trail maintained.

Restriction is "a less-common rights request but explicitly named in the NHSE IG guidance" and "distinct from the right-to-erasure." GV.PD-15's Limitations section notes "Many AVT vendors implement erasure but not restriction; deployers using such vendors will fail this metric until vendor capability catches up." This is a tooling gap independent from erasure—vendors may support one without the other.

**Recommendation:** Keep separate. Both are data subject rights; both are Tier 2. The dual measurement surfaces when vendors support only erasure, which would otherwise be hidden if merged.

---

### 5. GV.PD-17 (DPIA Justification Quality) vs GV.CR-7 (DPIA Template Completion Rate)

**Verdict:** Merge as parent + sub-parts (document relationship; keep separate sections)

**Rationale:**

- **GV.CR-7** tests structural completion: all eight mandatory sections of the NHSE template populated with substantive content; DPO sign-off dated within 30 days of go-live; DPIA-safety-case reconciliation documented.
- **GV.PD-17** tests substantive quality of one section: independent Caldicott Guardian review of the purpose-justification (Principle 1: "justify the purpose") specifically. Verdict: sufficient / needs revision / insufficient.

The taxonomy explicitly states: GV.PD-17 is "extending [GV.CR-7 DPIA Template Completion Rate], which tests structural completion" and "Caldicott Principle 1 ('justify the purpose') is the substantive test that completion alone cannot pass." The relationship is already named in the metric bodies (GV.PD-17 cross-links GV.CR-7; GV.CR-7 does not yet link back).

**Recommendation:** Formalize as parent + sub-part: GV.CR-7 is parent (structural completion gate), GV.PD-17 is sub-part (purpose-justification substantive review). No renumbering. Add explicit back-reference in GV.CR-7 body to GV.PD-17.

### 6. GV.CR-12 (Board-Level AI Governance Mechanism) — Overlap Check

**Verdict:** Distinct; no merge candidates identified

**Rationale:**

GV.CR-12 tests existence of a named board-level accountability point (committee or director) with documented AI oversight in formal remit, quarterly minimum cadence, and escalation path. This is a governance-structure metric. Examined for overlap against:

- **GV.SG cluster:** GV.SG-13 (Assurance Debt Accumulation Rate) and GV.SG-14 (Near-Miss Reporting Rate) are operational safety metrics, not governance structure.
- **GV.CR cluster:** GV.CR-6 (Clinical Safety Case Completeness) and GV.CR-7 (DPIA Template Completion Rate) are documentation/gating metrics. GV.CR-12 is the accountability *layer* above these.
- **Other governance:** CQC well-led, RAI Principle 10, and DTAC C3.1 all converge on this construct, indicating consolidation rather than fragmentation.

No existing metric measures board-level accountability for AI clinical systems pre-v5.3.0. GV.CR-12 fills a named gap; no merger is appropriate.

**Recommendation:** Keep as standalone Tier 1 metric.

## PART B: Emergent Named-Metric Families

The taxonomy currently has two named families: **Medication Safety Thread** (4 metrics, spanning summarisation/NLP → clinical coding → patient experience) and **Demographic Equity Disaggregation** (7 metrics, spanning ASR → coding → fairness). Assessed v5.3.0 metrics against four candidate families.

### 1. MHRA-Attestation Family?

**Candidate members:** GV.CR-11, GV.SG-18, GV.VT-9, GV.VT-10, GV.PD-12

**Verdict:** Keep separate; not a coherent family

**Rationale:**

All five metrics reference MHRA SaMD / AIaMD regulatory regime or Good Manufacturing Practice (GMLP) principle 3 (representative datasets). Examined for shared construct:

- **GV.CR-11** (Medical Device Classification Documentation): "Deployer must know regulatory status before go-live." Pre-deployment gate. MHRA Class I/IIa/IIb/III classification documented.
- **GV.SG-18** (PCCP Documentation Completeness): "PCCPs are the structural mechanism by which retrained or fine-tuned AVT models update without requiring a new regulatory submission per change." Adaptive model governance.
- **GV.VT-9** (Post-Market Surveillance Report Currency): "PMSR (Class I/IIa) availability on demand; PSUR (Class IIb/III) annual currency. Regulatory reporting cadence."
- **GV.VT-10** (MHRA Transparency Content Completeness): "Composite check of WHAT content items (device characterisation, performance, limitations, lifecycle)."
- **GV.PD-12** (Training Data Representativeness Documentation): "MHRA GMLP principle 3 (representative datasets)."

While all cite MHRA, the construct *unifying* them is not singular. GV.CR-11 is classification-documentation (a taxonomic gate). GV.SG-18 is model-change governance (an operational process). GV.VT-9 and GV.VT-10 are post-market reporting (compliance cadence + content). GV.PD-12 is fairness-readiness (bias mitigation upstream). No single parent-construct (e.g., "MHRA compliance", "regulatory evidence", "adaptive-model oversight") encompasses all five without being so broad as to be meaningless.

The MHRA citations are cross-cutting due to the regulator's broad scope over SaMD; they do not indicate a named family.

**Recommendation:** Keep separate. The MHRA reference is a shared *source* but not a shared *construct*.

### 2. NHSE IG Compliance Family?

**Candidate members:** GV.CR-13, GV.PD-13, GV.PD-14, GV.PD-15, + existing GV.CR-1, GV.CR-2, GV.CR-3, GV.CR-7, GV.PD-8, GV.PD-9

**Verdict:** Mint as **NHSE IG Compliance Family**

**Construct:** NHS England March 2026 Information Governance Guidance requirement alignment — documented compliance with NHSE IG-specific obligations on transparency, refusal handling, data subject rights, consent, and retention timing.

**Rationale:**

The four new v5.3.0 metrics are explicitly promoted from the NHSE IG gap-analysis roadmap (Gap-IG-A/B/C/D) in the taxonomy's Section 3.5.2 ("Gaps & Proposed Metrics"). Each is sourced as "[NHSE-IG-Guidance-2026-03]", the March 2026 guidance. The four are:

- **GV.CR-13** (Gap-IG-A): "Refusal impact-explanation quality … NHSE IG explicitly requires clinicians to explain *how* refusal affects care."
- **GV.PD-13** (Gap-IG-B): "Privacy notice currency & completeness … Organisational privacy notices must be updated to include ambient-scribe processing specifics."
- **GV.PD-14** (Gap-IG-C): "SAR deletion-pause interaction … Guidance explicitly requires deletion paused during active SAR handling."
- **GV.PD-15** (Gap-IG-D): "Right-to-restrict tooling support … IG guidance explicitly requires tool functionality for restriction."

These pair with existing metrics:

- **GV.CR-1** (Patient Dissent Recording): Respect for patient rights + refusal documentation (underpins GV.CR-13).
- **GV.CR-2** (Verbal Notification Compliance): Patient-facing transparency required by NHSE IG consent guidance.
- **GV.CR-3** (AI-Generated Content Labelling): Output disclosure + record-level transparency per NHSE IG.
- **GV.CR-7** (DPIA Template Completion): Mandatory DPIA per NHSE IG (March 2026 template named).
- **GV.PD-8** (Consent Verification Accuracy): Lawful basis + understanding, per CQC Mythbuster 109 + NHSE IG.
- **GV.PD-9** (Cross-Border Data Transfer Compliance): International lawfulness per NHSE IG data-flow mapping.

All ten are either sourced from NHSE IG guidance directly (the four new ones) or cross-referenced in the same guidance (the six existing ones). The shared construct is **documentation of compliance with NHSE IG-specific procedural and disclosure obligations**—not general GDPR, but NHS England's March 2026 operationalization of AVT governance.

**Recommendation:** Mint as named family.

**Proposed name:** NHSE IG Attestation

**Proposed family definition:** "Documentation and operational verification that AVT deployments comply with NHS England March 2026 Information Governance Guidance requirements on transparency, consent, data subject rights, and retention timing. Spans notification (GV.CR-2), dissent & refusal (GV.CR-1 / GV.CR-13), content labelling (GV.CR-3), consent (GV.PD-8), DPIA (GV.CR-7), cross-border transfer (GV.PD-9), privacy notice (GV.PD-13), SAR-deletion interaction (GV.PD-14), and right-to-restrict (GV.PD-15)."

**Member ref-IDs:** GV.CR-1, GV.CR-2, GV.CR-3, GV.CR-7, GV.CR-13, GV.PD-8, GV.PD-9, GV.PD-13, GV.PD-14, GV.PD-15

### 3. Caldicott / Consent Integrity Family?

**Candidate members:** GV.CR-14, GV.PD-17, + existing GV.CR-1, GV.CR-7, GV.PD-8, GV.PD-9

**Verdict:** Keep separate; insufficient coherence for named family

**Rationale:**

Examined for shared Caldicott principle alignment:

- **GV.CR-14** (Consultation-Type Appropriateness): Caldicott Principle 1 ("justify the purpose") + sensitive-consultation carve-outs (safeguarding, mental health, paediatrics, intimate exams, end-of-life).
- **GV.PD-17** (DPIA Justification Quality): Caldicott Principle 1 ("justify the purpose") + Caldicott Guardian substantive review.
- **GV.CR-1** (Dissent Recording): Respect for patient rights (Principle 4).
- **GV.CR-7** (DPIA Completion): Principle 1 structural completion (template sections).
- **GV.PD-8** (Consent Verification): Lawful basis + understanding (foundational to Principle 1).
- **GV.PD-9** (Cross-Border Transfer): Adequacy checks (Principle 5).

While GV.CR-14 and GV.PD-17 both cite Caldicott Principle 1 explicitly, the broader cluster (GV.CR-1 / GV.CR-7 / GV.PD-8 / GV.PD-9) spans Principles 1, 4, and 5 without a unifying construct. GV.CR-14 is about *which consultations* should be excluded from AVT (a carve-out assessment), whereas GV.PD-17 is about *why* AVT is processing (purpose justification)—different layers of Principle 1, not a shared family.

More fundamentally: the two "Caldicott-adjacent" metrics (GV.CR-14 and GV.PD-17) are already accounted for in the emergent NHSE IG family (GV.PD-17 extends GV.CR-7; GV.CR-14 is in the sensitive-consultation carve-out guidance from NHSE IG and Caldicott). Splitting them into a separate family would duplicate the relationship.

**Recommendation:** Keep separate. GV.CR-14 and GV.PD-17 belong in the NHSE IG Attestation family as part of the broader IG-compliance cluster.

### 4. Record Completeness & Semantic Structure Family?

**Candidate members:** TP.WB-8, + existing TP.WB-1, TP.WB-3, TP.WB-6

**Verdict:** Mint as **PRSB Semantic Completeness Family** (parent + sub-metrics)

**Construct:** EHR record fidelity across write-back pipeline — from output generation (fidelity) through field placement (mapping) through structural conformance (FHIR) to semantic/mandatory-element completeness (PRSB).

**Rationale:**

These four form a coherent stack:

- **TP.WB-1** (Write-back Fidelity): "Did the summarisation output get captured accurately in the EHR?" Tests whether content from AVT output is faithfully recorded (not lost, corrupted, or altered).
- **TP.WB-3** (Field Mapping Accuracy): "Are the structured fields in the AVT output mapped to the correct EHR fields?" Tests field-level routing and semantic alignment.
- **TP.WB-6** (FHIR R4 Conformance Rate): "Does the EHR data conform to FHIR R4 resource schema?" Tests structural validation.
- **TP.WB-8** (PRSB Semantic Completeness): "Are the mandatory information elements per PRSB standard present in the output?" Tests content completeness against the clinical-workflow-specific standard.

The family spans the write-back layer and directly addresses "Regulation 17 good governance — adequate records" (per TP.WB-8's rationale). All four are Tier 1 except TP.WB-6 (Tier 2), and the taxonomy cross-links them (TP.WB-6 and TP.WB-8 are described as paired for comprehensive coverage: "A deployer who passes structural FHIR validation but fails PRSB semantic completeness is producing records that are technically interoperable but clinically inadequate").

The FHIR vs PRSB distinction is crucial: FHIR R4 is structural (schema conformance), PRSB is clinical (mandatory-element presence per consultation type). Both are needed. TP.WB-8 is the highest-leverage metric in v5.3.0 per the taxonomy (cross-framework coverage: DTAC C4 + FHIR UK Core + CQC Regulation 17 + PRSB).

**Recommendation:** Mint as named family.

**Proposed name:** PRSB Semantic Completeness & Write-back Fidelity

**Proposed family definition:** "End-to-end write-back assurance from AVT output generation through EHR persistence — testing content fidelity (TP.WB-1), field mapping accuracy (TP.WB-3), structural FHIR conformance (TP.WB-6), and mandatory information element presence per PRSB standard and consultation type (TP.WB-8). The family spans output quality, integration correctness, and clinical adequacy of the final record."

**Member ref-IDs:** TP.WB-1, TP.WB-3, TP.WB-6, TP.WB-8
